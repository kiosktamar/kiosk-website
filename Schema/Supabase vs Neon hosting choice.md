# Keep Neon: Kiosk Tamar sleeps better there

**Stay with Neon Free, as the spec already says.** For a school kiosk that sits idle through vacations of weeks to months, the two free tiers fail in very different ways. Supabase Free **pauses a project after about 7 days of low activity, and only the owner can bring it back by clicking "Resume" in the dashboard** ([Supabase Docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)). Neon Free **suspends compute after 5 minutes idle and wakes it automatically on the next query**, typically "within a few hundred milliseconds" ([Neon Scale to Zero](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/scale-to-zero)). Neon's idle shutdown is in fact documented, in three official places, and cannot be turned off on Free. It just isn't a pause in Supabase's sense, because nobody has to act to undo it. On Supabase, every vacation turns into a keep-alive job you have to maintain, monitor and trust. Neon costs you a cold start of somewhere between a few hundred milliseconds and a few seconds on the first order after a gap, which a 15-second connect timeout and a retry absorb. Neon also comes out ahead on backups: Free has a 6-hour restore window plus one snapshot, while Supabase Free has no backups at all. Supabase cannot host the Next.js app in place of Vercel, so that option doesn't change the decision. Supabase becomes the better pick only if you actually build on its Realtime, Auth or Storage features, or if you pay for Pro.

## Neon suspends for minutes and wakes itself; Supabase pauses for good until you click Resume

The two behaviors look alike on a pricing page but work differently in practice. **Neon's scale-to-zero is documented and fixed on Free.** The plans page lists scale to zero as "After 5 min" with "Cannot disable" ([Neon Plans](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans)). The scale-to-zero page says "For Neon Free plan users, this setting is fixed" ([Neon Scale to Zero](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/scale-to-zero)). The pricing page says "Always enabled on Free (suspends after 5 min, cannot be turned off)" and adds that no compute hours accrue while suspended ([Neon Pricing](https://neon.com/pricing)). The compute "reactivates automatically" when a query arrives ([Neon Scale to Zero](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/scale-to-zero)), so a suspended Neon database behaves like a slow first request, not an outage.

**Supabase's pause is an outage until a human acts.** Free projects "that show low activity over a 7-day period" are paused. The signal is "user database activity", and "a few user requests to the database each day" is usually enough to avoid it ([Supabase Docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)). Supabase emails a warning roughly one week before the pause and a confirmation once it happens. Recovery is a manual restore in Supabase Studio, available for **1 year** after the pause ([Supabase Docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)). The 90-day window you may have read about comes from a June 2024 changelog and has been superseded ([Supabase Changelog](https://supabase.com/changelog/27497-paused-free-plan-projects-are-restorable-for-90-days)). After a year you can only download the backup and rebuild in a new project ([Supabase troubleshooting](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/troubleshooting/restore-project-after-90-days-pause)). A third-party guide puts restore time at two to five minutes for small projects ([SimpleBackups](https://simplebackups.com/blog/recover-paused-supabase-project)). No source describes traffic unpausing a project. For Kiosk Tamar, a summer break with no keep-alive means the first orders of the school year fail until you notice and resume the project.

**Neon has a long-idle mechanism of its own, branch archiving, and it also undoes itself.** Branches older than 14 days that haven't been accessed for 24 hours are moved to cheaper object storage. Unarchiving "happens automatically" on the next query, though branches with a lot of data may see "slightly slower connection and query times" while it runs ([Neon Branch archiving](https://neon.com/docs/guides/branch-archiving)). Expect the first query after a long vacation to be a bit slower than a normal cold start. A database this small should barely notice. I found **no policy that deletes inactive Free projects in Neon's AWS regions**, and the Free-limits FAQ says "None of these limits delete your data" ([Neon FAQ](https://neon.com/faqs/free-plan-limits-and-quotas)). There is one exception to know about. Free projects in Neon's deprecated Azure regions that have been inactive 90+ days "are subject to deletion as of October 5, 2026" ([Neon Regions](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/regions)). You can't create projects there anymore, but the rule shows Neon is willing to clean up idle free usage. That is the main reason to keep your own dumps even on Neon. Two points are uncertain: the docs don't say whether the default production branch can be archived, and Neon publishes no unarchive timings.

Ownership changes are a fair worry, since Neon joined Databricks in 2025 and is now branded "Lakebase Postgres on Neon" ([Neon docs](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/neon-and-lakebase)). So far the Free plan has only improved. The acquisition FAQ promised "The free plan stays" ([Neon Databricks FAQ](https://neon.tech/databricks-faq)). Free went from 20 to 30 projects in November 2025 ([Neon changelog](https://neon.com/docs/changelog/2025-11-07)) and now allows 100 projects with 1 GB per project ([Neon Plans](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans)).

## A daily write keeps Supabase awake, but only while nobody forgets it

If you choose Supabase, keep-alive jobs are a recognized and tolerated workaround. They are not a guarantee. Supabase's own docs list "making API calls to your project" as a way to head off a pending pause ([Supabase Docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)). The Acceptable Use Policy has nothing against pings. It prohibits bulk or automated account creation and use that "materially degrades" the service ([Supabase AUP](https://supabase.com/aup)). The evidence on what counts as activity comes from users, not from Supabase. One user's daily jobs that only opened a connection did not prevent deactivation. Another reported that a daily INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE kept a project alive from March 2024 through at least June 2025 ([GitHub Discussion #13121](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/13121)). Supabase publishes no exact threshold, so **write a row every day rather than ping once a week.**

The robust setup is a `KeepAlive` (or heartbeat) table and a Next.js Route Handler at `/api/cron/heartbeat`. The handler upserts a timestamp through Prisma, checks a `CRON_SECRET` header, and is marked `export const dynamic = "force-dynamic"`. Without that flag, a cached response means no query ever runs, a pitfall the popular travisvn/supabase-pause-prevention project warns about ([GitHub](https://github.com/travisvn/supabase-pause-prevention)). Schedule it with a once-daily Vercel Cron entry in `vercel.json`. On Hobby, Vercel cron jobs run at most once a day and at an imprecise time within the hour ([Vercel Limits](https://vercel.com/docs/limits)), which is fine for a daily heartbeat. A GitHub Actions schedule can be a backup trigger, but it has two documented weaknesses. Scheduled runs "can be delayed during periods of high loads", and in a **public** repository they are "automatically disabled when no repository activity has occurred in 60 days" ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/workflows-and-actions/events-that-trigger-workflows)). The second one is exactly what happens over a two-month summer with no commits. Use a private repo, or run Vercel Cron and the Action together.

Finally, treat the warning email as the alarm that tells you the keep-alive broke. Make sure the project owner's address is one you'll actually read during vacations, and add an inbox filter that flags any Supabase email about pausing. Even with all of this, you've added a moving part that exists only to work around a policy Supabase can change, and it fails silently, which is the worst way to fail.

## The free tiers are close on paper; backups and failure modes decide it

At Kiosk Tamar's scale, roughly 150 students placing a few orders a day, neither plan's storage or egress quota will come close to binding. The meaningful differences are in recovery, connectivity and what happens when something goes wrong.

| | Neon Free | Supabase Free |
|---|---|---|
| Idle behavior | Compute suspends after 5 min, auto-wakes ([Neon Plans](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans)) | Project pauses after ~7 days of low activity, manual resume ([Supabase Docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)) |
| Cold start | "Few hundred ms" official ([Neon latency](https://neon.com/docs/connect/connection-latency)); ~3 s in older community reports | None; always-on instance |
| DB storage | 1 GB/project; over it, writes blocked ([Neon Pricing](https://neon.com/pricing)) | 500 MB; over it, automatic read-only ([Supabase DB size](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/database-size)) |
| Egress | 5 GB/month; over it, compute suspended until next period ([Neon Pricing](https://neon.com/pricing)) | 5 GB + 5 GB cached; over it, grace period then restrictions ([Supabase Billing FAQ](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/billing-faq)) |
| Compute | 100 CU-hours/month, up to 2 CU ([Neon Plans](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans)) | Nano: shared CPU, 0.5 GB RAM ([Supabase Compute](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/compute-and-disk)) |
| Connections | 104 max at 0.25 CU; PgBouncer pooler up to 10,000 clients ([Neon pooling](https://neon.com/docs/connect/connection-pooling)) | 60 direct, 200 pooler clients ([Supabase Compute](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/compute-and-disk)) |
| Backups | 6-hour instant restore + 1 manual snapshot ([Neon Plans](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans)) | None; export it yourself ([Supabase Backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups)) |
| Frankfurt | `aws-eu-central-1` ([Neon Regions](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/regions)) | `eu-central-1` ([Supabase Regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)) |
| Extras | Auth up to 60k MAU ([Neon Pricing](https://neon.com/pricing)) | Auth 50k MAU, Storage 1 GB, Realtime, Edge Functions, Cron ([Supabase Pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)) |

**Connectivity is where Supabase has a trap specific to Vercel.** Supabase's direct host `db.[ref].supabase.co` is **IPv6-only** unless you buy the IPv4 add-on, which is Pro-only at about $4/month. Supabase itself lists Vercel and GitHub Actions as IPv4-only platforms ([Supabase IPv4](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/ipv4-address.md)). A Vercel deployment pointed at the direct host fails with `ENOTFOUND` ([Vercel Community](https://community.vercel.com/t/database-connection-from-vercel-to-supabase/25034)). Prisma's own Supabase guide still shows the direct host for `DIRECT_URL` ([Prisma docs](https://www.prisma.io/docs/orm/overview/databases/supabase)), which works only from IPv6-capable machines. On Supabase you would use the Supavisor transaction pooler (port 6543, `?pgbouncer=true`) for the app and the **session pooler** (port 5432 on `*.pooler.supabase.com`) for migrations. Transaction mode doesn't support prepared statements, session `SET`, advisory locks or LISTEN/NOTIFY ([Supabase connecting](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/connecting-to-postgres)). Neon's pooler has nearly the same transaction-mode restrictions ([Neon pooling](https://neon.com/docs/connect/connection-pooling)), so neither choice frees you from those. Neon's setup is just more direct: a pooled `DATABASE_URL` whose host contains `-pooler`, and an unpooled `DATABASE_URL_UNPOOLED` for `prisma migrate` ([Neon Prisma guide](https://neon.com/docs/guides/prisma)). A lunch rush of 40 users fits comfortably within either plan's pooler limits.

**Overage behavior matters more than the quotas themselves.** On Neon, running out of CU-hours or egress means "Existing connections drop and new ones can't open" until the next monthly period ([Neon FAQ](https://neon.com/faqs/free-plan-limits-and-quotas)). That would be a mid-month outage, and the one realistic way to cause it is a too-frequent ping that keeps compute awake. A ping every 4 minutes keeps 0.25 CU running about 180 hours a month, roughly 45 CU-hours. That is under the 100 CU-hour cap but pointless. So **don't run a keep-alive on Neon.** Supabase is gentler on quota overage, with a grace period before restrictions ([Supabase Billing FAQ](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/billing-faq)), but that hardly matters when the inactivity pause is the far more likely failure.

**Backups are the quiet tiebreaker.** Supabase backs up only Pro and higher plans and tells Free users to run `supabase db dump` and keep copies off-site ([Supabase Backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups)). Neon's 6-hour window lets you undo a bad migration the same afternoon. A mistake noticed the next morning needs the snapshot or your own dump. You should run scheduled `pg_dump`s on either platform. The difference is that on Neon they are a safety net, while on Supabase they are your only backup.

## Supabase cannot host the Next.js app, so Vercel stays

Supabase has no frontend hosting product. The long-running static-hosting request is officially "not on the roadmap", and a maintainer said they were investing in the Vercel integration instead ([GitHub #991](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/991)). Storage serves HTML as plain text to block phishing ([GitHub #2557](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/2557)). Edge Functions run Deno with a **2-second CPU limit per request**, and they convert HTML to plain text unless you use a custom domain ([Supabase Edge limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/limits)). The newly announced Supabase Compute is a private-alpha waitlist product aimed at agent sandboxes and backend services, with unpublished pricing ([Supabase Compute](https://supabase.com/compute); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/10/02/database-startup-supabase-raises-150m-acquires-turso/)).

Going "all on Supabase" would mean a static React SPA hosted somewhere else, talking to Supabase directly through supabase-js. Business logic would move into RLS policies and PL/pgSQL functions, for example a `place_order` RPC that locks and decrements stock. Prisma and your Route Handlers would go away. That is a large rewrite, and it moves the server-side classes and services your 10-unit project is graded on into SQL. That trade is bad for an OOP-focused final project. Phone login on Supabase Auth also needs a paid SMS provider ([Supabase Phone Login](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/phone-login)). And the 7-day pause still applies.

Vercel Hobby has no idle sleep. It includes 1M function invocations and 4 active-CPU hours a month, and it is restricted to "non-commercial, personal use" ([Vercel Hobby](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby)). A free school kiosk fits that description. If the app ever collects money, read Vercel's fair-use terms first. Among the alternatives, Render's free tier sleeps after 15 minutes and takes about a minute to wake, and its free Postgres expires after 30 days ([Render](https://render.com/docs/free)). Netlify pauses a site once its 300 monthly credits are used up ([Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/pricing/)). Cloudflare Workers can run the App Router through OpenNext, but the 3 MiB free worker limit makes a Next.js + Prisma bundle risky ([OpenNext](https://opennext.js.org/cloudflare)). Vercel remains the right host.

## Setting up Neon so cold starts never show as errors

Create the project in **AWS Frankfurt (`aws-eu-central-1`)** and set the Vercel function region to `fra1` so the app and database sit close together. Use two connection strings, as Neon's Prisma guide prescribes ([Neon Prisma guide](https://neon.com/docs/guides/prisma)). The first is `DATABASE_URL`, the pooled `-pooler` host, used by Prisma Client through `@prisma/adapter-neon` (Prisma 7). The second is `DATABASE_URL_UNPOOLED`, the direct host, which `prisma.config.ts` uses for migrations. Add `connect_timeout=15&pool_timeout=15` to the connection string. Neon's guide says cold-start failures usually mean "the Prisma query engine timed out before Neon activated the compute" ([Neon Prisma guide](https://neon.com/docs/guides/prisma)). Wrap the order-submission path in a small retry with backoff. Neon's own example uses 5 attempts with a 4-second minimum delay ([Neon latency](https://neon.com/docs/connect/connection-latency)), which is generous for a kiosk, so 2–3 attempts are enough. Show a "connecting…" state rather than an error on the first slow request. Adding `sslnegotiation=direct` shaved about 120 ms off a connection in Neon's example ([Neon latency](https://neon.com/docs/connect/connection-latency)), if your driver supports it.

For data safety, take Neon's one manual snapshot before every risky migration. Run a weekly `pg_dump` against the unpooled URL from a **private-repo** GitHub Action or your own machine, and always take one before vacations. That covers the remaining risk that Neon's long-inactivity policy, which is currently silent for AWS, changes. Open the Neon console once a month to check CU-hour and egress usage. Don't add any keep-alive or warm-up ping. At lunch the compute wakes on the first order and stays warm while the rush continues, because the gaps between orders are shorter than 5 minutes.

**Switch to Supabase if** you decide to use Supabase Realtime for live order-status screens, Supabase Storage for menu images, or Supabase Auth instead of your own auth. Those features are included free, and Neon doesn't match them ([Supabase Pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Switching also makes sense if the school will pay $25/month for Pro, which never pauses and adds daily backups ([Supabase Pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Even then, keep Prisma on the pooler URLs and run the daily-write heartbeat described above if you stay on Free.

## Conclusion

For a usage pattern of short bursts and long silences, the useful question isn't which free tier is bigger. It's what happens on the first school day after the summer, when nobody is watching. On Neon the answer is a first order that takes a few hundred milliseconds to a few seconds longer than usual. On Supabase Free it is an outage that lasts until you remember to resume the project, unless a cron job you wrote months earlier kept running correctly the whole time. Supabase's Realtime and Auth extras are real, but Kiosk Tamar's architecture (Prisma, Route Handlers, its own service layer) doesn't use them, so you would take on Supabase's maintenance cost without getting its benefits.

The decision also comes with a clear argument for your project documentation. You can explain that you chose scale-to-zero over an always-on instance because the school's usage is intermittent, and that you designed for cold starts with timeouts and retries instead of working around a pause policy. That shows the kind of engineering reasoning a final project is supposed to demonstrate. Two uncertainties remain. Neon could add an inactivity policy for AWS like the Azure one, and real-world cold-start figures for 2025–2026 are missing. A routine `pg_dump` covers the first, and a quick timing test from Vercel in `fra1` during your first week of testing settles the second.
